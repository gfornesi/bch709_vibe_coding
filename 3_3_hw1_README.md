Explain 2 possible biological reasons for the GC content distribution pattern in yeast mRNA:
There are two main peaks in the GC content graph. The first is at about 0.4 and the second is at about 0.55. One reason for this distribution pattern is gene expression. Because Gs and Cs have three hydrogen bonds holding them together instead of two like As and Ts, areas with a higher GC content are more difficult to separate and transcribe. This distribution could show that less genes have lower transcription rates, indicated by the smaller second peak, and more genes have higher transcription rates, indicated by the larger first peak.  
Another reason for the GC content distribution pattern is the temperature in which the yeast are adapted to. Because this yeast is not adapted to very hot temperatures, it has a lower GC ratio. In very hot environments, a higher GC ratio is needed, as the DNA is more stable and has a higher melting point. This higher melting point is due to Gs and Cs having three hydrogen bonds holding themselves together instead of As and Ts having two. Three hydrogen bonds take more energy or heat to break than two hydrogen bonds.
